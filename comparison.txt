Nearest Neighbour 
1.) This algorithm just simply maps the cordinate to the original matrix cordinate by rounding off
no complex mechanism. (Literally copying the colours)

2.) If we have a look at the evidence , in the matrix generated using neighourest neighbour , we can 
see duplicate RGB blocks such as [229 230 232] repeating acros multiple rows and columns.

3.) Visually , the process is computationally fast becuase there's no complex math involved , when it 
comes to the image , when zoomed in i could see a difference in the image compared to the other. it
has a "blocky" or pixelated look.

Bilinear Interpolation

1.) this method maps the target cordinate , finds the finds the exact fractional distances ($dx, dy$), and calculates 
a weighted average of the four closest surrounding pixels

2.) When compared to the nearest neighbour this , algorithms produces a matrix , that has less repeating
blocks compared to the other,the RGB values gradually step down: [19 19 18] blends into [16 16 14] and then [14 14 11].

3.) Well computationally much heeavier takes more time , when looking at the image , the pixel colour distribution
is different , edges appear much softer and more natural, though the image might look slightly blurry compared to the original.


It was bit hard to spot the difference in the sample image i used at first , but when i zoomed the sample one and 
the , two produced by the algorithm , there was a clear noticeable difference on how the pixels were coloured.

Upscaled and sample images of the nelum kuluna is also included , with zoomed screenshots to spot 
the difference.
